I learn best when there is a real system in front of me.

I am not trying to present myself as a finished specialist. I am a cybersecurity graduate with practical experience, a strong interest in computers and a habit of turning curiosity into working projects.

That has meant building a PC, hosting personal cloud storage, setting up media services, writing diagnostic scripts, making websites and learning how to explain technical issues clearly.

Where the interest started

My interest in computing has always been broad rather than locked to one narrow area. I enjoy hardware, websites, troubleshooting, software, servers and the security side of how systems should be handled.

Building a whole desktop PC from individual parts made the physical side of computers feel much less abstract. Hosting my own services then did the same for networking, storage and remote access.

How university shaped it

My Cyber Security degree gave structure to that interest. The strongest parts for me were the subjects connected to networks, malware, databases, online risks and system behaviour.

Projects involving Python, Packet Tracer and virus or antivirus behaviour pushed me to be more accurate, because the work had to match actual technical requirements instead of just looking finished.

How I want to use it

I am aiming for roles where I can be useful while still learning: IT support, technical support, junior infrastructure, cyber security support or web development.

I care about understanding the issue, writing down what was checked, communicating clearly and leaving the next person with something usable.

Working style

Try the obvious checks first, avoid guessing, document the result, then narrow the problem.

Personal detail

Cat person, home-server tinkerer and the sort of person who will spend too long fixing a setup just because it should work properly.
